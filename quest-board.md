THE QUEST BOARD — design brief for a first-draft mockup

CONTEXT
A private web dashboard. Exactly one person will ever see it. It is the home
screen of a solo bookkeeping practice, re-skinned as a tavern quest board. No
client will ever lay eyes on it, so it can be as thematic as it likes.

LOOK
Warm tavern interior. Dark wood and stone, iron lanterns with orange flame,
candlelight, deep shadow at the edges. Gold and violet dominant. Aged parchment,
wax seals, hand-cut pins. Lived-in, not clean. Painterly and warm rather than
flat and modern — closer to a game menu than a SaaS dashboard.

LAYOUT

1. THE MAIN QUEST — pinned at the top, always present, never scrolls away.
   A large aged-parchment notice: the current month's close, "the 15th," and
   days remaining as a plain number. A gold coin motif, because it is the only
   thing on this board that actually pays. It does NOT pulse, blink, redden, or
   escalate as the date approaches. It is simply always there.

2. ED — a framed portrait at the left: the innkeeper behind his bar, wry and
   unbothered. Beside him, one speech panel containing ONE line: the single next
   thing to do, named, with a first step. Never a list. He is the only voice on
   the page.

3. THE QUEST LOG — the board itself. Pinned notes on wood, ordered but never
   numbered. Visually distinct types: main quest line (parchment, gold wax
   seal), side quests (smaller notes pinned at angles), dailies (small, plain,
   deliberately low-contrast — present but quiet), repeatables (a small
   recurring mark). Nothing carries a percentage. An old quest just looks old:
   slightly yellowed, corner curling. That is the only aging signal permitted.

4. THE SHIELD — a crest mounted on the wall with time remaining shown on it.
   Dull metal when inactive, glowing when active. Visible either way.

5. THE LISTENER — a small faceted crystal on fine chains in the corner of the
   board. Glows gold when a record is waiting to be triaged; dark and unlit when
   there is nothing. This is the only notification on the entire page.

HARD RULES — these are the point, not style preferences
- Nothing is ever marked overdue. No red, no "LATE", no exclamation marks.
  Anything past due simply appears as today's.
- No counters of any kind. No tasks-completed, no streaks, no daily totals, no
  progress rings.
- No failure state for anything, ever.
- One voice: only Ed speaks. Nothing else on the page addresses the user.
- One ask at a time: Ed's panel holds exactly one instruction.

MOOD
Warm, lived-in, faintly ridiculous, entirely unbothered. It should feel like
walking into a pub where someone is glad to see you and mildly annoyed you're
late — in that order.
